The principle

Two audio directions. One modular wired system.

Communication points are connected to the central controller. Audio can be captured, processed and transmitted in both directions at the same time, while the architecture leaves room for additional participants.

01 / CAPTURE

Capture speech

Microphones at both communication points capture speech independently.

02 / PROCESS

Process both directions

The controller filters and processes the audio paths centrally.

03 / OUTPUT

Hear and speak

Participants can receive audio while the system remains ready to transmit.

04 / EXPAND

Expand the system

The architecture is designed as a modular concept and can be extended with additional communication points.

Wired vs Wireless

Why add a cable when wireless exists?

Wireless technology is useful, but a permanently installed vehicle intercom has different priorities. A dedicated wired link provides a defined communication path.

Wireless

Potential wireless limitations

Wireless communication adds additional variables to the signal path.

Radio interference Shared radio spectrum can be affected by other systems.
Pairing & reconnecting Additional connection management may be required.
Variable latency Buffering and radio conditions can introduce delay.
More dependencies Radio modules and protocols become part of the signal path.
OpenBoardIntercom

A defined physical connection

The communication path is permanently available and physically defined.

No pairing Participants are permanently connected.
Full duplex Both directions can operate simultaneously.
Central processing Audio processing happens in one defined controller.
Predictable operation No radio reconnection is required for the audio link.

Modular architecture

Two participants today.
More when needed.

The basic topology uses driver and passenger as two communication points. The system concept is not limited to this arrangement: additional participant nodes can be integrated into an extended architecture where required.

The exact number of supported nodes depends on the final controller and hardware implementation.
